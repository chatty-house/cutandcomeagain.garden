the doctrine

Sweet peas are a cut-and-come-again flower. Leave the blooms on the stem and the plant sets seed, decides its work is done, and stops. Pick them — every few days, ruthlessly, with gratitude — and it goes on flowering for the whole season.

A correspondence works the same way. It only stays alive while someone keeps collecting the letters. And it returns next season only if somebody saved the seed.

That is the whole philosophy of this plot. There are no rooms here to get lost in; for rooms, visit the house next door. A garden keeps nothing indoors. It keeps a gate, a bench, and an address.
